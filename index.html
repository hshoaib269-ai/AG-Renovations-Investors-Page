Our Strategy

We only buy where value can be created, not just paid for.

Every deal has at least two levers we can pull to raise value. Buying for cash lets us move quickly on the right stock. Doing the refurbishment ourselves lets us control the three costs that decide whether a deal works: labour, materials and time.

1

Acquire the right stock

We target short-lease flats, repossessions, probate sales and uninhabitable properties, where the seller is motivated and the market is thin. Cash purchase means we move fast, close on our terms, and avoid mortgage-driven timelines.

2

Extend the lease

Where the lease is short, we extend it. A statutory 90-year extension on a flat with under 80 years remaining is often the single biggest value uplift on the deal, and one that most buyers won't or can't do.

3

Reconfigure the layout

Rooms that do not work for a modern buyer get redrawn. Two-bed conversions, dual-aspect living space, better circulation, an en-suite where the layout allows. Small re-planning moves change how a flat feels far more than their cost suggests.

4

Fully refurbish, in-house

Bathrooms, kitchens, electrics, plumbing and decoration are all delivered by our own team. We are not an investor paying a builder. We are the builder, which is what lets us hold cost, quality and programme where an outside investor cannot.

5

Sell into a clean market

We present each finished property with professional staging, agent-ready photography, and clear compliance documentation (Gas Safe, electrical, warranty). Priced against local comps to sell within 8–12 weeks.

6

Recycle the capital

Proceeds from each sale roll into the next acquisition, one deal at a time. No over-leverage, no exposure spread across too many concurrent projects, and no compromise on the finish.

The Market Gap

We deliberately buy where the crowd cannot follow.

Every property we target is unmortgageable at the point of purchase, whether through a short lease, poor condition, probate or repossession. That one fact removes most of our competition. First-time buyers cannot borrow against them, chain buyers cannot proceed on them, and most investors are not set up to fix them. A cash buyer with its own renovation team has this market largely to itself.

Once the lease is extended, the layout reworked and the refurbishment finished, the same flat is mortgageable and ready to move into. That opens it to first-time buyers, the largest and most active segment of the UK market, with a clean legal pack and nothing left to do. We buy where competition is thin and sell where demand is deep.

Why Now

The London market suits our model right now.

Interest rates, flat transaction volumes and a growing pool of short-lease flats have combined to produce motivated sellers and unusually thin bidding on the stock we buy.

Signal 01

Short-lease supply

Rising numbers of London leasehold flats now sit under 80 years, off-limits to most mortgage lenders, but ideal for a cash buyer with lease-extension expertise.

Signal 02

Auction repossessions

Repossessions and forced sales are trickling back into UK auctions after the rate-cycle shift, providing consistent access to below-market stock.

Signal 03

Probate backlog

The probate backlog has left many properties inherited but unmodernised, with executors motivated to sell and few competing bidders.

Signal 04

Refurb cost inflation

Refurbishment costs have risen faster than sale prices, which punishes amateur flippers and rewards anyone who controls labour and materials directly.
